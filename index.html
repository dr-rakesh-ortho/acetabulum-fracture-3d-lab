Model provenance, limitations, and clinical references

Bone geometry: NIH 3D, 3DPX-015682, version 2. Weissbrod, E (HJF), Jones, E (USU), Mateo, RB (USU); “3D Models of the Male Pelvis with L4, L5 Vertebrae and Proximal Femur”, Department of Surgery, USUHS, Bethesda, MD. Licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (attribution, noncommercial, share alike). The left fracture model mirrors the right reference hip and its cuts; it is not an independently segmented left fracture. The right hip, context left hip and sacrum use this entry's STL files. Source scale is 100%, units mm. No rescaling, anatomical sculpting or mesh decimation has been applied. Duplicate/degenerate triangles and non-volume fragments were cleaned for Boolean cutting. The right hip has 95,364 triangles before fracture cuts. Pelvic-ring context uses approximate rigid placement of the separate source bones; it is not a validated joint assembly.

Fractures are newly constructed, simplified solid cuts on reference anatomy, not CT-derived injuries or expert-validated patterns. Wall fragments use curved cutting volumes; column patterns use planar cuts. Each pattern shows one representative configuration. Marginal impaction, comminution, cartilage, vessels, nerves, ligaments, and collision constraints are absent. The instrument is reconstructed from your reference photograph, including curved jaws, pointed ball tips, a hinge, finger rings and a threaded locking spindle. It is not measured manufacturer CAD. Its length is adjustable; 240 mm is an initial assumption. Contact points do not certify a safe surgical trajectory. Review fracture morphology and contact placement with an acetabular surgeon before teaching from them.

Actual Stoppa exposure depends on dissection, retraction, anatomy and associated windows. The restricted Stoppa mode uses an opaque schematic soft-tissue cover with an approximate exposure boundary mapped from your supplied shaded photograph. It covers the iliac wing and inferior bone while revealing a band along the brim, superior pubic ramus and quadrilateral surface. The outline stays attached to the fixed anatomical reference while fragments move beneath it. This is a projected visibility mask, not segmented muscle anatomy, a measured surgical corridor, or a guarantee of direct articular visualization. The quoted measurements in the photograph are not treated as patient-specific dimensions. Full medial view remains available for comparison. Oblique presets are orientation aids, not diagnostic X-ray projections.
